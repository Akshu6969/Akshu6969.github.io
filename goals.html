Career Roadmap

My ultimate professional aspiration is to become a Systems Architect, bridging the critical gap between intricate hardware design and highly scalable software solutions. As a Computer Engineering student, I am passionate about understanding systems from the silicon level all the way up to user-facing applications, enabling me to design products that are both technically optimized and deeply integrated.
Phase I: 1 - 2 Years

Skill Acquisition & Industry Exposure

The immediate focus is to translate my academic coursework and independent lab projects into production-level industry experience.

  • Industry Internships: Secure software engineering or embedded systems internships to gain hands-on experience with agile development cycles and industry-standard version control.
  • Advanced Prototyping: Continue expanding my independent portfolio by building complex cross-disciplinary projects, focusing on microcontrollers (ESP32, ARM) and test-driven development frameworks like Google Test.
  • Tool Mastery: Deepen my proficiency in low-level languages (C, C++, RISC-V) while maintaining a strong grasp of scalable backend architectures.
Phase II: 3 - 5 Years

Academic Completion & Professional Foundation

This phase centers on formalizing my education and establishing myself as a highly capable technical contributor.

  • Degree Completion: Graduate with a Bachelor of Science in Computer Engineering from the Georgia Institute of Technology, having completed advanced coursework in systems architecture and digital signal processing.
  • Full-Time Integration: Transition into a full-time role as an Embedded Software Engineer or Backend Systems Developer.
  • Hardware-Software Co-Design: Master the challenges of production-level hardware limitations, memory manipulation, and real-time operating systems (RTOS) to write perfectly optimized code.
Phase III: 5+ Years

Systems Architecture Leadership

By leveraging my comprehensive understanding of both physical constraints and software scalability, I aim to transition into architectural leadership.

  • Technical Strategy: Drive the technical direction for complex, integrated product development, such as advanced IoT infrastructures or consumer hardware ecosystems.
  • Cross-Functional Leadership: Lead and mentor engineering teams, acting as the technical translator between hardware engineers, software developers, and product managers.
  • Innovation: Architect robust systems that balance extreme performance requirements with user experience, maintaining high reliability at scale.
